# Getting started with Episteme

Episteme is an assistant that answers questions about the University of Benin
using the university's own documents. Ask a question in plain language and it
retrieves the relevant material before answering.

## What it can answer

Admissions requirements and processes, academic policies and regulations, course
and programme information, fees and financial aid, and general university
information — to the extent those have been loaded by your institution.

It also answers questions about itself: how to use it, and what it can and
cannot do.

## What it will not do

It does not answer general-knowledge questions, give personal advice, or help
with anything outside university information and the platform itself.

More importantly, it does not answer from memory. Every factual statement is
drawn from a retrieved document and carries a citation you can follow to the
source. Where it has no verified document, it says so and offers to try a
different angle rather than producing a plausible answer.

## Reading the answer

Numbered markers in the text link to the sources listed beneath it, with the
title and date of each. Following one takes you to the document the claim came
from.

Two things are worth attending to:

**Dates.** If a source is more than a year old, the answer says so and advises
verifying with the relevant office. Take that seriously for anything that
changes — fees, deadlines, office holders, and calendars.

**Unverified sources.** When nothing in the university's own documents answers a
question, the assistant may fall back to a public web source, and will state
plainly that the result is not verified against official records. Treat those
differently from cited institutional documents.

## Getting better answers

Ask for the specific thing rather than the topic. "Fees" gives the assistant no
angle and it will ask which aspect you mean; "when is the deadline to pay second
semester fees" can be retrieved directly.

You do not need to repeat your programme or level — it already has your details
and uses them to narrow the search. You can ask about a different programme or
level explicitly when you want to.

Follow-up questions work: "what happens if I miss it" after a question about a
deadline is understood in context.

## When it says it has no information

That means no document matching your question was found — not that no answer
exists. Accepting one of the suggested alternatives is usually faster than
rephrasing, since each is a genuinely different retrieval angle. If none of them
finds it, the material has probably not been loaded yet, and the relevant office
is the right next step.
